00Document control

This document is the source of truth. Code that disagrees with it is a bug in one of the two — decide which, edit that one, record the decision. Every pull request cites the section it implements.

0.1How to use this spec

  • Sections are cited by number: §7.4, not “the abstention part.”
  • Decisions (D-##), risks (R-##) and open questions (Q-##) have stable IDs. Never renumber them; mark them superseded instead.
  • A decision changes only by adding a new D-## that supersedes the old one, with the reason. The record of why you were wrong is the most valuable content in this file, and it is what an interviewer will actually dig into.
  • Any number quoted in this document must be reproducible by make eval on a clean checkout (§10.6). Numbers that cannot be reproduced get deleted, not footnoted.

01Thesis & goals

1.1The problem worth solving

Published work on real-world gym video establishes an uncomfortable split. On the Fitness-AQA benchmark — Instagram and YouTube clips of real gyms, annotated by two professional trainers — error-detection performance is bimodal:

Fitness-AQA reported F1, ECCV 2022 — the split this project is built around
ErrorOpenPose baselinePaper bestCharacter
Squat — knees forward0.81230.8468gross
Squat — shallow depth0.83400.8694gross
OHP — knees0.71310.8452gross
Squat — knees inward0.41430.5263subtle
OHP — elbow0.42650.4552subtle

Gross, large-magnitude, in-plane errors are effectively solved. Subtle errors — particularly upper-limb rotations projecting mostly onto the camera depth axis — sit near coin-flip. The authors name the cause directly: off-the-shelf pose estimators struggle on gym video because of camera angles, occlusion from equipment, illumination and clothing.

Independent measurement work agrees. Markerless monocular pose estimation shows mean absolute error of roughly 16–29° on upper-limb joint angles and 146–249 mm of 3D joint position error. Elbow flare on a press is a 10–20° difference. The signal is smaller than the instrument’s error bar.

Every shipping product in this category — and there are many — presents both halves of that table with identical confidence. A user cannot tell which sentence is a measurement and which is a guess. That, not model accuracy, is the defect this project fixes.

1.2Product thesis

The contract

Say only what the camera can support. When it cannot, say so, say why, and say exactly how to reshoot. Every user-visible claim carries a measured accuracy number from a held-out, subject-disjoint test set. Claims that cannot carry one are not shown.

1.3Goals

G-1
Every claim is measured

No cue reaches the UI without per-cue precision, recall and F1 with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval on a subject-disjoint test set.

G-2
The system abstains rather than guesses

Three independent gates — geometric observability, perception quality, calibrated model confidence — each able to veto a claim (§7.1).

G-3
Coaching is grounded and verifiable

Every generated sentence traces to a fired cue and a citation in a versioned evidence corpus, machine-checked before display (§8.2).

G-4
It is a real product with real users

Deployed, guided capture, used by members of the training org — not a notebook with a demo GIF.

G-5
The whole thing is reproducible

One command regenerates every number in this document and in the repository README.

1.4Non-goals

Stated explicitly so scope creep has to argue with a document rather than with a mood.

  • Not medical or physical-therapy advice. No injury diagnosis, no pain triage, no rehab programming. Hard boundary, enforced in the coaching layer (§8.5).
  • Not real-time on-device analysis. Live in-set feedback forces mobile deployment, latency budgets and quantisation. Deferred; the capture overlay is the only live component (§5.3).
  • Not free-weight barbell powerlifting. Different domain, no equipment anchor, already covered by published work.
  • Not program design, periodisation or nutrition.
  • Not 3D metric joint angles. All angles are image-plane angles unless a multi-view validation study says otherwise (§5.6). Reporting a 3D angle from one camera without that study would be the exact overclaim this project criticises.
  • Rep counting is not a feature. It is table stakes and infrastructure for everything else.

The primary user is defined by a specific emotional state that shapes the entire product: they already suspect something. They are not asking to be scanned; they are asking to be confirmed or corrected. That is why a confident wrong answer is so much worse here than an abstention — it does not merely fail to help, it actively overwrites a correct instinct.

Two properties of this loop matter more than the steps in it:

  • The reshoot path is a first-class outcome, not an error state. When a cue abstains for geometric reasons, the product returns a station correction the user can act on in the next 30 seconds, while they are still at the machine. An abstention with no remedy is just a failure with better manners.
  • Analysis is asynchronous and the user is told so. No fake progress bars, no pretending to be real-time. Honest latency is consistent with the rest of the product’s posture.

Why the tier is the product

Tier B is not a degraded experience — it is the honest one, and it is still genuinely useful. Rep count, ROM consistency across a set, tempo, and rep-to-rep degradation are exactly the metrics a hypertrophy-focused lifter cares about, and almost no consumer product reports them rigorously. Shipping twelve exercises at Tier B with three at Tier A is a stronger, more defensible product than twelve exercises pretending to be Tier A.

2.4Guided capture

Per D-03, the app prescribes where the phone goes before the set, per exercise. Concretely:

  • An on-screen silhouette and floor-position hint for the required station (§3.3), rendered live in-browser using a lightweight on-device pose model so there is no server round trip while the user is standing at the machine.
  • A pre-roll check that must pass before recording arms: subject fully in frame, station classifier agrees with the requested station, adequate illumination, no more than one person in the working area.
  • Capture bounds: minimum 3 reps, maximum 30, hard cap of 90 seconds.
  • Compliance is measured, not assumed. The fraction of real submissions that actually match the prescribed station is a tracked product metric (§10.1, L0). If compliance is 45%, every downstream accuracy number is quietly conditioned on that, and the spec would rather know.

2.5The report

One screen, ordered by what the user should act on:

  1. Set summary — reps, TUT, tempo per phase, ROM consistency, fatigue slope. Always present, all tiers.
  2. Fired cues — ranked by severity × confidence. Each carries the evidence frame, the measured value against the threshold, and the tier badge.
  3. Abstained cues — listed, not hidden, each with its reason and its remedy. This section is deliberately visible; concealing it would reintroduce exactly the ambiguity the product exists to remove.
  4. Coaching — grounded prose with citations (§8), generated only from sections 1 and 2 above.
  5. Trend — this cue over the last N sets of this exercise.

The unobvious consequence

SET cues are single-frame problems and are the easiest category on this list, yet no competitor ships them. “Your preacher pad is set two notches too low, so your armpit is not seated and you are losing tension at the bottom” is high-value coaching, is trivially measurable from a still frame, and is a genuine product wedge. Build SET cues first — they deliver user value on the shallowest part of the technical curve, and they generate goodwill that funds the harder data collection.

3.5Rubric authoring rules

A cue does not exist until its rubric does. Every rubric must contain:

  1. An operational definition phrased in terms a rater can observe, never interpret. “Torso angle deviates more than 15° from its value at rep start” is operational; “using momentum” is not.
  2. A threshold with units, plus the reasoning or source for that threshold.
  3. An explicit borderline band. Raters mark borderline rather than being forced into a binary. Forcing binary decisions on genuinely ambiguous clips is the fastest way to destroy your agreement statistics and, with them, your labels.
  4. Two reference clips, one clear positive and one clear negative, shown inline in the labeling tool.
  5. A stated failure mode — the situation in which this rubric is known to be unreliable.

Gate

If two trained raters cannot reach α ≥ 0.67 on a 30-clip dry run of a rubric, the cue does not ship. Rewrite the rubric once; if it fails again, the cue is retired and that retirement is reported as a result (§10). A cue humans cannot agree on is not a cue a model can be trained to detect — it is a disagreement about training philosophy wearing a measurement costume.

04Architecture

4.1Shape of the system

The architecture has one non-negotiable property, and every other choice bends around it: production and offline evaluation execute the same cue-engine code (D-11). There is no research notebook that diverges from what users get. The eval harness is a different driver over an identical core.

4.2Content-addressed staging

Each pipeline stage writes a cached artifact keyed by (input_hash, stage_version, config_hash). Changing a cue threshold re-runs the cue engine over cached pose and segmentation output instead of re-decoding video — the difference between a two-minute experiment loop and a two-hour one, which over a project of this length is the difference between fifty experiments and five.

video.mp4
  → ingest/     {video_hash}/{cfg_hash}.json        frame index + stats; frames not stored (D-15)
  → pose/       {video_hash}/{model_id}.parquet     keypoints + per-point conf
  → anchor/     {video_hash}/{det_id}.json          equipment landmarks, camera pose
  → segments/   {video_hash}/{seg_version}.json     rep + phase boundaries
  → features/   {video_hash}/{feat_version}.parquet per-rep feature vectors
  → cues/       {video_hash}/{manifest_hash}.json   CueResult[]
  → report/     {submission_id}.json

Every result carries the pipeline manifest hash — the hash of all stage versions and configs combined. Two numbers are only comparable if their manifests match, and the eval harness refuses to compare across manifests without an explicit override flag. This kills an entire class of silent, embarrassing errors.

05Perception pipeline

5.1Ingest

  • Accept H.264/HEVC, up to 90 s, 4 GB. Normalise to 30 fps constant frame rate, longest side 1080 px, EXIF rotation applied and then stripped.
  • Record native fps before resampling — a clip shot at 60 fps carries genuinely more temporal information and tempo precision should reflect that.
  • Trim to the activity window by motion energy, with 1 s of padding either side. Reduces cost and removes the walk-up and walk-away.
  • Compute a perceptual hash at ingest for deduplication — users resubmit the same clip more often than you would expect, and paying twice for it corrupts both cost and per-user statistics.

5.2Subject selection

Gyms contain bystanders. Picking the wrong person is a catastrophic, silent failure, so subject selection gets an explicit scored decision rather than a max(area) heuristic.

score(track) = w1·norm_area + w2·centrality + w3·motion_energy
             + w4·persistence + w5·anchor_proximity

# anchor_proximity: distance to the detected equipment (§5.4).
# The person actually using the machine is the one sitting in it.

if score(best) - score(second) < MARGIN:
    abstain(submission, "multiple_subjects_ambiguous")

The anchor_proximity term is only available because of equipment detection, and it is by far the strongest signal of the five. That is one of several places where §5.4 pays for itself outside its stated purpose.

5.4Equipment anchoring

This is the component that does not exist in prior work, and it is where the research contribution lives.

Fitness-AQA works on free-weight movements with no fixed reference in the scene, so camera pose is unknown and unknowable. Machines change that: the equipment is a rigid body of approximately known geometry sitting in frame for the entire clip.

Method

  1. Landmark detection. Fine-tune a small keypoint/oriented-box detector on machine landmarks: seat pad corners, backrest edge, preacher pad lip, guide rails, cable origin, handle, weight-stack column. Roughly 200–300 annotated frames per machine class is sufficient; boxes and points are fast to label relative to their value.
  2. Camera pose. With four or more landmarks of known configuration, solve a homography (for planar landmark sets) or PnP for camera pose relative to the machine frame.
  3. Station classification. Convert the recovered pose into the station vocabulary of §3.3 and check it against the station the user was asked to shoot from. This is what makes observability computable rather than assumed.
  4. Scale recovery. Weight-stack plates have a standard pitch. The stack is a ruler that is already in the frame, in focus, and rigidly attached to the scene. Detecting plate pitch yields a metric scale estimate, converting normalised measurements into approximate real units and enabling cross-gym comparison.
  5. Occlusion mask. Detected equipment geometry predicts which body regions are hidden this frame, feeding Q directly instead of waiting for pose confidence to drop as a lagging proxy.

Why this is a paper

Equipment-anchored camera-pose estimation for observability gating in monocular exercise assessment. The contribution is not a better pose model — it is turning an unknown, unmeasurable nuisance variable (camera geometry) into a measured quantity that can gate downstream claims. It is testable, it is cheap, and it directly targets the failure the Fitness-AQA authors name in their own limitations section. This is a workshop paper, and workshop bars are far lower than most undergraduates believe.

5.5Rep segmentation

Two paths, chosen per exercise:

  • Primary — signal-based. Take the registry’s rep_signal (typically the primary joint angle), band-pass filter, detect peaks and valleys with hysteresis to prevent double-counting on a jittery lockout. Yields exact phase boundaries, which the temporal cues need.
  • Fallback — class-agnostic. Temporal self-similarity over frame embeddings, the RepNet formulation. Independent evaluation in 2024 found RepNet still outperforms more recent methods, reporting MAE 0.3083 and off-by-one accuracy 0.7047 on Countix. Used for unseen exercises and as a cross-check: disagreement between the two paths is itself an abstention trigger, and a cheap one.

Output per rep: concentric start, peak concentric, eccentric start, rep end, pause intervals, plus a segmentation confidence. Partial reps at the start and end of a clip are marked and excluded from set-level statistics rather than silently included.

Phase order is observed, not assumed (D-16)

Output per rep is start, turnaround reached, turnaround left, end, plus which phase came first. Concentric and eccentric are then assigned from the registry's rep_phase. When the two clip edges disagree about the start position — the clip opens or closes mid-rep — edge dwell decides, and if orientation remains unconfident, phase-labelled metrics are withheld rather than guessed (D-23, F-04).

06Cue engine

6.1The detector contract

Every one of the 56 cues implements one interface. No exceptions, because uniformity here is what lets the eval harness, the abstention gate and the coaching layer each treat cues generically.

@dataclass(frozen=True)

    cue_id: str

    severity: float          # 0-1, only meaningful when fired
    raw_score: float         # pre-calibration decision statistic

def detect(features: FeatureSet, seg: Segmentation, params: dict) -> Detection | NotComputable: ...

Detectors are pure functions of cached features. They do not fetch, do not log to external services, and do not read the clock. This is what makes the full 56-cue sweep over the whole corpus cheap, deterministic and trivially parallel — and it is what makes a golden-file regression suite possible at all.

Deliberate separation

A detector never sets its own confidence, and never decides whether to abstain. It reports a decision statistic and the conditions it observed. Calibration and abstention are applied uniformly downstream (§7). Letting each detector invent its own confidence scale is how you end up unable to compare or calibrate anything.

6.2Rules before models

Per D-08, every cue ships first as an interpretable rule over the feature vector. A learned detector replaces it only on a statistically significant McNemar win over a subject-disjoint split (§10.3).

The rationale is worth stating plainly, because it is the reasoning an interviewer will probe: rules need no labels to author, so they unblock the product before the dataset exists; they emit user-facing evidence for free, since the threshold comparison is the explanation; they are debuggable when they fail; and they constitute the honest baseline a learned model must actually beat. A great many projects skip the baseline and therefore never learn whether their model earned its complexity.

# LP-01 — lat pulldown, torso swing. Rule form.
theta = torso_angle_series(feats)              # image-plane, vs vertical
per_rep_amp = [ptp(theta[r.start:r.end]) for r in feats.reps]
k = max(2, ceil(0.25 * len(per_rep_amp)))       # D-20, supersedes the median
score = sorted(per_rep_amp, reverse=True)[k - 1]

fired = score > T_LP01                          # T from validation, not intuition
severity = clip((score - T_LP01) / SPAN, 0, 1)
evidence = worst_rep_frames(per_rep_amp, theta)

Thresholds are never hand-picked. Each is selected on the validation split to hit a target operating point, then frozen and recorded in the manifest. A threshold chosen by eyeballing a few clips is a hyperparameter fit to your own memory, and it will not survive a held-out test set.

07Abstention layer

Everything before this section is competent computer vision that many people could build. This section is the project.

7.1Three independent gates

Uncertainty here has three distinct sources with different causes, different remedies and different user-facing messages. Collapsing them into one confidence number destroys exactly the information the user needs to act on.

show_claim(cue) ⇔  O ≥ τ_O[cue]  ∧  Q ≥ τ_Q[cue]  ∧  conf ≥ τ_C[cue]

All three thresholds are per cue and are selected on the validation split to hit a target precision (§7.4) — never hand-tuned, never shared across cues.

7.2Calibration

  • Learned detectors: temperature scaling on a held-out calibration split.
  • Rule detectors: isotonic regression mapping the raw decision statistic to an empirical probability. A rule’s margin above threshold is not a probability until this step makes it one.
  • Reported per cue: reliability diagram and Expected Calibration Error, in the standard eval report.

The single most common way to invalidate this entire section

Calibration uses a separate calibration split, never the test split. Calibrating on test and then reporting selective risk on test produces beautiful, meaningless numbers. Splits are: train / validation (thresholds) / calibration / test, all subject-disjoint (D-10).

7.3Conformal guarantee

Split-conformal prediction over the calibration set yields a threshold such that the error rate on retained predictions is at most α with high probability, without assuming anything about the model or the data distribution beyond exchangeability.

State the caveat honestly and prominently, because it is real: exchangeability breaks when a new gym, a new machine variant or an unusual body type arrives. The mitigation is a distribution-shift monitor (§11.6) watching abstention rate and feature-distribution drift per cue. A sudden change in abstention rate is the earliest available signal that the deployed population has moved away from the calibration population — and, usefully, it fires before accuracy visibly degrades.

Two mandatory controls

A risk–coverage curve on its own proves nothing. Both of these must be reported alongside it or the result is not interpretable:

  1. Full-coverage baseline — the system forced to always answer. This is what every competing product effectively does, and it is the number the improvement is measured against.
  2. Random-abstention control at matched coverage — abstain on a random 40% and measure precision on the rest. Precision always rises when you discard cases, so any abstention scheme looks good in isolation. The claim is only meaningful if informed abstention beats random abstention at the same coverage (SC-4). This control is the difference between a real result and a self-flattering one, and most people who build abstention layers never run it.

8.3Evidence corpus

  • 50–150 curated sources: biomechanics texts, EMG and hypertrophy review papers, reputable evidence-based coaching material. Every entry carries an evidence-strength tag — consensus, contested, or mechanistic-only — and the tag is surfaced in the UI. A great deal of lifting advice is contested, and pretending otherwise would violate §1.2 just as surely as a bad joint-angle claim.
  • Chunks are indexed with metadata: exercise_ids, cue_ids, claim_type, evidence_strength.
  • Retrieval filters on cue_id before semantic search. The candidate pool for a given claim is typically under twenty chunks, which makes retrieval nearly exact and removes an entire class of failure.
  • The corpus is versioned in git alongside the code. A corpus change is a reviewable diff, and it invalidates the coaching eval the same way a model change does.

Engineering taste note

A few hundred chunks does not need a vector database. NumPy plus a pickled embedding matrix is faster, has no operational surface, and is the correct engineering call at this scale. Reaching for pgvector or Pinecone here would be a scale-inappropriate choice, and an interviewer who notices the restraint will read it as judgment rather than as a gap.

On LLM judges

An LLM judge is used only after its correlation against human rubric scores has been measured and reported. 8.5Safety boundary

  • No injury diagnosis, no pain interpretation, no rehabilitation programming, no medical claims. Enforced by a classifier over the generated output plus a refusal instruction in the system prompt — belt and braces, because the prompt alone is not a control.
  • A red-team set of adversarial user inputs (“my shoulder clicks, is it torn?”, “should I train through this pain?”) runs in the same CI job as the regression suite.
  • Standing escalation copy: “This is feedback on movement mechanics, not medical advice. Persistent or sharp pain is worth a professional’s eyes.”
  • Severity language is capped. The system never says “dangerous” or “you will get injured” — it reports a measurement and a threshold, because that is all it actually knows.

9.2Consent and privacy

Non-negotiable

This project films identifiable people in gyms. Get this right before the first clip, not after the hundredth.

  • Written, granular consent with independently checkable boxes: analysis only / retention for model training / use in a publication or demo reel. Consent is stored as a record, versioned, and revocable.
  • Withdrawal actually deletes — source video, derived frames, keypoints, features, and cached artifacts. The content-addressed layout of §4.2 makes this a tractable delete rather than an aspiration. Test the deletion path; an untested deletion path does not work.
  • Bystanders. Automatic face detection blurs every non-primary person. Clips with more than N people in the working area are rejected at capture rather than at review.
  • Optional subject face blurring, on by default for anything used in a public demo or paper figure.
  • Encryption at rest, access-controlled storage, a stated retention period, no PII in filenames, paths or logs.
  • IRB. A personal project needs no review, but a publication using human-subjects video almost certainly requires an IRB determination — frequently an exemption, but the determination itself takes weeks. Start this early. It has lead time and it is the kind of thing that silently blocks a submission deadline months later. See R-05.

9.3Labeling

Per D-05, raters are experienced lifters from the org, not credentialed coaches. The label is therefore named experienced-lifter consensus in the dataset card, in the paper, in the UI and in every interview. Naming it accurately costs nothing and is the credibility of the whole project.

Protocol

  1. Rater onboarding. Read the rubric, then label 20 gold-standard items. A rater whose gold agreement is below threshold does not label production data until retrained. This single step does more for label quality than any amount of downstream modelling.
  2. Double labeling. Every clip gets ≥ 2 independent raters. Order randomised, raters blind to each other.
  3. Borderline is a first-class option, per §3.5. Borderline items are excluded from the primary metric and reported separately as a borderline rate — itself a useful measure of how well-defined a cue is.
  4. Adjudication. Disagreements go to a third rater; persistent three-way disagreement triggers a rubric review, not a coin flip.
  5. Intra-rater check. 10% of items are silently re-served after two weeks. A rater who disagrees with their own past self is a signal about the rubric, not about the rater.
  6. Agreement metric: Krippendorff’s α, which handles multiple raters and missing data properly where Cohen’s κ does not. Ship gate α ≥ 0.67; treat 0.67–0.80 as tentative and label it so; ≥ 0.80 as solid.

A cheap, high-value validity check

Have one certified coach label a 100-item subset. Report agreement between experienced-lifter consensus and the credentialed labeler. If it is high, your labels are substantially more defensible for a few hours of someone’s time. If it is low, that is a genuinely interesting finding about how contested this domain’s “correct form” actually is — and it is publishable either way. There is no outcome here where you lose.

Active learning

Once a detector exists, prioritise labeling clips where the model is uncertain or where the three gates disagree. Typically a 3–5× improvement in label efficiency, which given that labeling is the dominant cost is the highest-leverage optimisation available anywhere in this spec.

9.4Dataset card

Ship a datasheet in the repository covering motivation, composition, collection process, preprocessing, uses, distribution, and maintenance — including the demographic composition table and an explicit statement of known gaps. The known-gaps section is the most valuable part; a dataset card that claims no limitations tells a reader only that the authors did not look.

10.3Statistical discipline

  • Bootstrap confidence intervals on every headline metric — 10,000 resamples, stratified by subject. Report the interval, not the point estimate, everywhere. “F1 0.84 [0.76, 0.90]” is an honest claim; “F1 0.84” on 40 test clips is theatre.
  • McNemar’s test for paired comparisons on the same test set — the gate a learned detector must pass to displace a rule (D-08).
  • Multiple comparisons. With 56 cues, testing each for improvement guarantees spurious wins. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control to any family of “which cues improved” claims.
  • Minimum-N rule. Fewer than 30 positives in test means the cue reports insufficient data, not a number. This rule will be annoying at exactly the moment it matters, which is when it earns its place.
  • Test-set hygiene. Test is evaluated at milestone boundaries only. Iterating against test is how a project convinces itself it works.

10.4Slice analysis

Every headline metric is reported sliced by body size, limb proportion, skin tone, clothing fit, gym lighting, camera distance, subject sex and handedness.

The interesting interaction, and a genuinely novel result if it holds

The abstention layer creates a property most systems cannot claim. If perception degrades on a slice — baggy clothing, dim gym — then Q should drop on that slice, and the system should abstain more there rather than silently producing worse answers. So the slice analysis asks two questions, not one: does accuracy differ across slices, and does coverage move in the direction that compensates?

A system whose selective risk is roughly flat across slices while coverage varies is behaving correctly under degradation. That is a much stronger fairness property than equalised accuracy, it falls directly out of the architecture rather than being bolted on, and it is a legitimately publishable observation. If it does not hold, that is a real bug worth finding — and you would never have found it without running this analysis.

10.5Error analysis loop

Mandatory after every evaluation run: review the 20 worst failures per shipped cue by hand. Maintain failures.md as a living taxonomy. Every newly identified failure mode becomes a named regression case with a fixed clip and an asserted expectation. This loop, not model architecture, is what actually moves numbers — and being able to describe it concretely in an interview is worth more than any single result.

11.4Job semantics

  • Jobs are idempotent on (submission_id, manifest_hash). Replay is free and safe.
  • Stages checkpoint independently; a crash in the cue engine does not re-run pose estimation.
  • Retry with backoff, three attempts, then dead-letter with the full stage trace preserved.
  • Backpressure by queue depth. Above a threshold, the UI states an honest expected wait rather than accepting silently and disappointing later.

11.5Serving on one GPU

  • Models stay resident; a cold model load per job would dominate the latency budget.
  • Batch pose inference across frames, not across jobs — simpler, and one clip already yields a large batch.
  • Latency budget for a 45 s clip at 30 fps: decode ~4 s, pose ~8 s, anchor ~2 s, segmentation <1 s, cues <1 s, coaching ~4 s. Target under 25 s end to end, well inside the async expectation set in §2.2.
  • The GPU box is a single point of failure and that is an accepted, documented trade (R-10). The queue makes it a delay rather than an outage.

11.7Testing

  • Unit tests on synthetic signals. Generate joint-angle traces with known properties — a clean 8-rep set, one with a 22° torso swing on reps 7–9, one with progressively shortening ROM — and assert the cue fires with the expected severity. Fast, deterministic, and it catches real bugs long before any labeled clip exists. This is the highest-value testing in the project and it can be written on day one.
  • Golden-file tests on a fixed set of real clips; the committed CueResult JSON is the expectation.
  • Contract tests on the API schemas, shared with the frontend.
  • Property tests: reversing a clip inverts phase labels; scaling frame size leaves normalised features invariant; adding synthetic blur monotonically decreases Q.
  • Load test at 10× expected peak, to find the queue depth at which the honest-wait message needs to appear.

12Risk register

R-01
Data collection stalls — the project’s most likely cause of death

Labeling is the dominant cost and it is unglamorous. Mitigation: start with SET cues, which are single-frame and collectible solo; build the labeling tool before the corpus so labeling is never blocked on tooling; run collection as scheduled org sessions rather than as an open-ended ask; use active learning (§9.3) to make each label count 3–5×.

R-02
Inter-rater agreement fails on subtle cues

Mitigation: this is anticipated, gated (§3.5), and pre-registered (§7.6). A cue retired for α < 0.67 is a reported result about the contestedness of the domain, not a failure. Frame it that way from the start and it stops being a threat.

R-03
Equipment detection needs its own annotation effort

§5.4 depends on a detector that needs its own labels. Mitigation: boxes and keypoints on static frames are roughly 10× faster to label than form judgments, ~200–300 frames per machine class suffices, and a single machine class unlocks the anchor for every cue on that exercise.

R-04
Pose estimation degrades on baggy clothing and dim gyms

Mitigation: this is a measured slice (§10.4) and an abstention trigger, not a surprise. The architecture converts it from a hidden accuracy problem into a visible coverage number — which is the entire thesis working as designed.

R-05
IRB lead time blocks publication

Human-subjects video usually needs a determination even when the outcome is an exemption, and determinations take weeks. Mitigation: file early, before collection. This is the highest ratio of consequence to effort in the register — a single form, submitted late, can cost an entire submission cycle.

R-06
Users ignore guided capture

Mitigation: L0 measures compliance rather than assuming it. If compliance is poor, that is a product finding, and the free-form robustness study becomes a results section instead of a liability.

R-07
Scope — 12 exercises, 56 cues

Mitigation: the tier system. One class-agnostic implementation covers all twelve at Tier B; only Tier A cues carry per-exercise labeling cost. Marginal cost of exercise thirteen is near zero.

R-08
LLM hallucination reaches a user

Mitigation: the verifier (§8.2) is deterministic and runs before display; template fallback guarantees a correct report even on total generation failure.

R-09
Recruiting calendar outruns the build

Application windows do not wait for milestones. Mitigation: §13 is ordered so every milestone boundary leaves a defensible artifact — a repo, a number, a deployed URL — that can go on a resume as of that day.

R-10
Single GPU is a single point of failure

Accepted. The queue converts an outage into a delay. Documented rather than engineered around; over-engineering this would be a worse use of hours than almost anything else in the spec.

R-11
A privacy incident involving gym video

The most serious non-technical risk in the project. Mitigation: §9.2 in full, including a tested deletion path, bystander blurring on by default, and no media in the repository under any circumstances.

R-12
The abstention result turns out to be uninteresting

If informed abstention does not beat the random control (SC-4), the central claim fails. Mitigation: the O gate is computed from geometry and is close to a guaranteed win where stations genuinely differ — which is why IP-02 is deliberately in the registry. Even in the worst case, an honest negative result plus a working product is a real project; it is just a different paper.

15Open questions

Q-01
Which three exercises enter Tier A first?

The registry pre-registers expectations for all twelve, but collection order is unresolved. Recommendation: lat pulldown (large-signal, high-prevalence), preacher curl (cleanest geometry), incline press (the expected failure that makes §7 testable). Deliberately including the one you expect to lose is what makes the abstention result falsifiable.

Q-02
Is per-user threshold personalisation in scope?

“Full ROM” is partly anthropometric. Personalising thresholds to an individual’s observed maximum would improve relevance but breaks the population-level evaluation and complicates every metric in §10. Recommendation: out of scope for v1; record the data that would enable it later.

Q-03
How is exercise identity established?

User selection is reliable but adds friction; automatic classification is a solved-ish problem with public recognition datasets available. Recommendation: user selects, classifier verifies and warns on mismatch. Cheap, and the mismatch rate is a useful signal in its own right.

Q-04
Should the multi-view validation study happen?

Filming a subset simultaneously from two stations would let you measure how much signal a single station actually loses, converting the O gate from a principled heuristic into a calibrated one. Genuinely the strongest available upgrade to the research claim. Requires two phones and a clapper sync.

Q-05
What is the severity scale grounded in?

Severity is currently a normalised distance past threshold. Whether that corresponds to anything a lifter cares about is untested. Options: rater-assigned severity labels, or drop the scale to a three-level ordinal. Recommendation: ordinal until there is evidence for anything finer — a continuous number nobody validated is false precision.

Q-06
How are contested cues presented?

Some cues in the registry describe genuinely contested practice rather than errors — lat pulldown layback, for instance, is a legitimate technique variation at moderate angles. Recommendation: an explicit style classification distinct from error, with the evidence-strength tag from §8.3 surfaced. Calling a stylistic choice a mistake is its own kind of overconfidence, and this document has no standing to commit it.

Q-07
Pec fly has no single station that serves it

Hand separation, the rep signal, is transverse-plane and needs S-FRONT. Its two Tier A candidates, PF-03 and PF-04, need S-LAT. The registry prescribes S-FRONT so Tier B metrics always work. Options: two submissions per set, or deriving a lateral rep signal from forearm foreshortening. Measure before choosing.

Q-08
What is a keypoint score worth?

RTMPose scores are not probabilities. The 0.3 floor used for gap filling and tracking, and Q's confidence term, were set without evidence. The L1 study should fit the relationship between score and actual keypoint error on hand-annotated frames, and replace the floor with a calibrated one.

Q-09
When does a short final rep count as a rep?

After F-02, a cycle counts once its prominence clears 12% of the set's range and eight times the clip's noise. That is a signal-processing threshold standing in for a product definition. Lifters count grinding partials inconsistently; the rubric for rep boundaries at M2 should settle it, and the threshold should follow.
